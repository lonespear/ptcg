# PLAYBOOK — the agent's strategy, as an auditable table

*Every strategic behavior modifier in the agent is an entry here, or it is not
strategy. Each entry carries three mandatory fields: the **measurement** that
learned it, the **behavior** it drives, and the **gate** it passed before
shipping. Entries a judge can recompute. (Austin's ledger: D23, D25.)*

## Rules of the table

- Append-only; a superseded entry gets a strikethrough and a successor, never deletion.
- "Gate result" is a number with a game count, or the entry does not ship.
- Negative results stay in the table struck through — they are evidence too
  (see: hysteresis margin 500, −14 pts; ES/TD weight tuning, null vs defaults).
- 2-ply has now been refused three times, and the three refusals are one
  argument rather than three data points. The first blamed the opponent model,
  the second blamed the determinization, the third had both fixed — a table
  counted off 2.7M field decisions that covered every position it was asked
  about — and lost anyway, while moving a tenth of the decisions. Deepening the
  search is not the lever while the leaf is a 1-ply linear margin. The next
  attempt on this axis has to improve the leaf first.
- The tree leaf is the sharpest instance of this table's oldest rule, and the
  two screening arms are what identify it. A forest fitted with the two struck
  hand features withheld reads **0.3593 over 295** (Marnie mirror, same seeds,
  `tree_leaf_nohand.json`) — better than the full forest's 0.2485 and still a
  rout. A forest restricted to **exactly the seven terms the shipped linear
  evaluator scores**, whose weights were each screened as decision weights
  before they shipped, reads **0.3379 over 293**. So the collapse is not the
  feature set. It is the functional form, used in a place that constrains what
  a functional form is allowed to do.
  A linear evaluator gives every feature one global sign. That constraint is
  not a limitation the forest escapes — it is the thing that makes a fitted
  description safe to spend decisions on. Each of the six shipped weights was
  screened as a decision weight (and the hand differential was screened and
  struck, at 0.360, for failing exactly that test). A 419-tree forest carries
  hundreds of implicit local weights, none screened, and it inherits every
  description-vs-decision inversion in the mined data at once — in local
  regions where the linear fit was forced to pick one sign and the screen
  could see it. TreeSHAP names the largest of them: over the 201 searches
  where the two leaves disagree, `hand_diff` moves the tree's pick by
  **+0.174 log-odds** on average, the largest term by a factor of four, which
  is the struck row reappearing as a shape rather than as a coefficient.
- Two things the tree leaf is NOT, both measured, because a diagnosis that
  skips them is a story. It is not a resolution problem: over 615 live
  searches averaging 6.69 candidates, the forest produced **3.872 distinct
  values a search against the linear margin's 3.548**, and tied at the top on
  **46.7% of searches against 60.3%** — the step function discriminates
  between near-identical candidates more finely, not less, and the tie-
  breaking hypothesis is refused. Nor did it starve the search: the override
  rate with the leaf on is **0.517** (60 of 116), against the 0.5038 the
  shipped build runs at, so the search is still firing and still overriding —
  it is overriding toward worse moves. What it does cost is time: **0.083 ms
  an evaluation against the linear margin's 0.008**, and **18.51 ms a
  main-menu decision against 12.60**. Under the 1 ms bar, and not the reason
  it lost.
- Agreement between the two leaves is load-dependent and has to be quoted with
  its condition. The search is deadline-bounded, so a busy machine completes
  fewer determinizations and the equal-sample filter admits fewer candidates:
  the same ten games read **67.3% agreement over 615 searches** idle and
  **55.8% over 684** with five other workers running. Both gate arms ran in
  the same processes under the same load, so the A/B is unaffected; the
  agreement statistic is not, and the idle number is the one quoted above.
- A fitted coefficient is a description of positions winners reach. It earns a
  decision weight only if spending the resource produces the advantage it
  measures — which is what the struck hand-differential row cost us to learn.
  Entry 6 is the same lesson read off the sign: the exposure count's pooled
  interval excludes zero and points the wrong way, because positions with a
  Pokemon in range are positions a developed board is in.
- Three hundred games decides nothing on the specialist arm. Entry 5/6's
  combined configuration read **+8.1 pp at 300 games and −3.5 pp at 900**, same
  seeds, same build, the second sample a superset of the first. Every number
  quoted from that harness is the 900-game one, and the 300-game reads are in
  this bullet rather than in the table because their only use is as a warning.
- A specialist arm has to say who forfeited. The harvested Grimmsnarl agent's
  last-resort fallback returns an empty selection whenever the prompt carries
  `maxCount` 0, and it hands over about a third of its games that way — 174 of
  300 before `specialist_gate.py` made the deck-selection call that resets its
  per-episode state, and roughly a third after. Scored over all games that
  reads as 0.86 for whoever is opposite it and moves with its crash rate
  rather than with our play, so the entries above quote the games neither side
  forfeited and print both.
- Mirror gates under-measure matchup features by construction. Both seats play
  one deck, so a deny-posture keyed on the opponent's archetype fires against
  our own — and for two of the eight archetypes POSTURES.md says there is no
  lever there at all. C2 hit the same wall from the other side (its term is
  inert on 98% of mirror positions). A matchup entry needs a panel, not a
  mirror, and one specialist is not a panel either.
  `scripts/posture_panel_gate.py` is now that panel — the eight field entries
  at fitness play-weights, 89.8% of the weight under matched specialists on
  their own lists — and entry 5's second refusal is its first reading.
- Two things about C2 a reader should know before trusting its gate. The
  margin it adds is worth 39 on average and 126 at one standard deviation over
  the 14,000 mined positions, against a C3 table fitted for the vector without
  it; that staleness changes the behind/ahead verdict on **1.19%** of those
  positions at the shipped k=3 weight (167 of 14,000), and moving from k=2 to
  k=3 accounts for 52 of them, **0.37%** — which is why the table was not
  refitted for either. And the term is inert in
  an exact mirror of our own Ogerpon list — both sides afford the same best
  attack, so the differential is 0 on 98% of positions — while against the
  field it fires on 98–100%. The mirror gate therefore measures it under the
  one matchup where it has least to say.
