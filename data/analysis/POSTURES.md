Machine-readable spec: `data/analysis/postures.json`, generated by `python -m ptcg.matchup_postures`. Offline, `ptcg.matchup_postures.apply(archetype)` returns a full WEIGHTS dict for `JonDayPilot(search=True, weights=...)`.

The agent can read that file itself, and the integration is built: `agent/main.py::_refresh_traj_arch` takes the posterior's top archetype and its confidence once a decision and hands both to `_set_posture`, which folds the bench delta into the vector `_margin` reads and loads the named gust order; `_rank_card_options` replaces the generic score on opponent-owned named targets with the spec's order. Each spec's `gust_order` field carries the same preference the `scoring_rule` states in prose, as card ids, so the integration reads the order instead of parsing English.

## What our action space can and cannot deny

Our list (agent/deck.csv, Teal Mask Ogerpon ex) has one attacker whose damage lands only on the Active (Myriad Leaf Shower, 30 + 30 per Energy on both Actives), Boss's Orders x3, Judge x4, Harlequin x2, Tool Scrapper x1. So:

- We cannot touch their draws, searches, or abilities directly — no ability lock, no item lock, no bench damage.
- The one real targeting lever is **Boss's Orders + KO**: gust a 30–110 HP engine body off their bench and take it. That denies the future draws/evolutions/acceleration the body would have produced — the thing section C priced.
- **Judge x4** is the only draw-volume counter, and hand size is not a term in the evaluator, so Judge prioritization needs card-score integration in main.py, not a weight.
- The evaluator has no per-target term. A weight-delta can pay for "their bench got smaller" (`WEIGHTS["bench"]`) but cannot prefer Munkidori over Impidimp. Named gust preference orders are written in each spec's `scoring_rule` for the integration pass.
- Weights act only through the forward search; rules-only play reads them just for the behind/ahead trigger. Postures require `search=True`.

Delta sizing: `bench_delta = 20 x deny_pp_per_sd x lever_factor` (lever_factor 1 = cheap gustable engine bodies, 0.5 = component partly in unreachable trainers, 0 = no lever). The largest delta (~178) roughly doubles the default bench weight of 153; none of them outbids a prize (1000).

## Screening probe (not a gate)

One posture validated offline: anti-Grimmsnarl (bench 153 -> 325) on the creation harness, our list with `JonDayPilot(search=True, weights=apply(...))` vs the harvested grimmsnarl specialist on its own list, 200 games per arm, seat-swapped, 4 workers. Result in `data/analysis/probe_grimmsnarl_posture.json`:

| arm | games | win rate | 1 SE | loss modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (defaults) | 200 | 0.740 | 0.031 | no-active 31, prizes 21 |
| posture (bench 325) | 200 | 0.785 | 0.029 | no-active 27, prizes 16 |

Delta +4.5 pp with an SE on the difference of ~4.2 pp — directionally positive, roughly one standard error, and it did not backfire (both loss modes shrank). A screening read that says the lever is worth a gated test, not that it works.

Caveats that keep this a screening read: one specialist opponent is not the ladder population; 200 games per arm resolves only ~±3.5 pp at 1 SE; and the specialist's own-deck play is stronger than the median ladder pilot but stylistically narrow. A real gate would be the SPRT harness against the full specialist panel.
